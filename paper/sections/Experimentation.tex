\section*{Experimental Results}

This section details the experimental setup and evaluation of SaltNet's denoising performance in comparison to existing state-of-the-art methods. We conducted a comprehensive evaluation across various noise types, noise levels, and benchmark datasets.

\subsection{Datasets}

The performance of SaltNet was evaluated on four public datasets \textbf{BSD68}\cite{Martin01}, \textbf{Kodak24}\cite{Kodak}, \textbf{Urban100}\cite{Huang2015}, and \textbf{Set12}\cite{Set12}.

\subsection{Noise Simulation and Levels}

Images from the four utilized datasets were corrupted with one of four distinct types of noise: \textbf{Poisson Noise}, \textbf{Bernoulli Noise}, and \textbf{Salt and Pepper Noise} with noise density ranging from 150/255 to 240/255 in increments of 10/255. And \textbf{Gaussian Noise}: with noise levels (standard deviation $\sigma$) were set to 15/255, 25/255, 50/255, and 60/255.

Detailed experimental results of SaltNet in comparison with Restormer are listed, both SaltNet and Restormer were trained using the methodology outlined in the training section \ref{sec:training_details}. Both models were trained on the same training data, same hardware and for the same number of epochs. The denoising performance was quantitatively evaluated using the \textbf{Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR)} and \textbf{Structural Similarity Index (SSIM)} metrics. PSNR focusing on pixel-level fidelity and SSIM emphasizing structural and perceptual aspects. We found SaltNet's denoising performance is competitive with Restormer's.

SaltNet demonstrates higher computational efficiency, evident through the lower memory usage on our testing hardware. While Restormer was able to restore images up to 1024x1024 pixels before going out of memory, SaltNet was able to restore 2048x2048 images on the same hardware. We have also calculated the throughput of each model in images/sec, the results show that SaltNet could be a better fit for high throughput or video applications.

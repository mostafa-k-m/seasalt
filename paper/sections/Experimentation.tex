This section details the experimental setup and evaluation of MPDenoiseNet's denoising performance in comparison to existing state-of-the-art methods. We conducted a comprehensive evaluation across various noise types, noise levels, and benchmark datasets.

\subsection{Datasets}

The performance of MPDenoiseNet was evaluated on four public datasets \textbf{BSD68}\cite{Martin01}, \textbf{Kodak24}\cite{Kodak}, \textbf{Urban100}\cite{Huang2015}, and \textbf{Set12}\cite{Set12}.

\subsection{Noise Simulation and Levels}

Images from the four utilized datasets were corrupted with one of four distinct types of noise: \textbf{Poisson Noise}, \textbf{Bernoulli Noise}, and \textbf{Salt-and-Pepper Noise} with noise density ranging from 150/255 to 240/255 in increments of 10/255. And \textbf{Gaussian Noise}: with noise levels (standard deviation $\sigma$) were set to 15/255, 25/255, 50/255, and 60/255.

Detailed experimental results of MPDenoiseNet in comparison with Restormer are listed, both MPDenoiseNet and Restormer were trained using the methodology outlined in the training section \ref{sec:training_details}. Both models were trained on the same training data, same hardware and for the same number of epochs. The denoising performance was quantitatively evaluated using the \textbf{Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR)} and \textbf{Structural Similarity Index (SSIM)} metrics. PSNR focuses on pixel-level fidelity and SSIM emphasizing structural and perceptual aspects. We found MPDenoiseNets denoising performance is competitive with Restormer's.

MPDenoiseNet demonstrates higher computational efficiency, evident through the lower memory usage on our testing hardware. We have also calculated the throughput of each model in images/sec, the results show that MPDenoiseNet could be a better fit for high throughput or video applications.

Traditional image denoising methods often rely on handcrafted filters or optimization algorithms, which may not generalize well to different noise types. Deep learning-based approaches have proven to be powerful in image denoising tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on a variety of noise types.

One of the pioneering works in deep learning-based image denoising is DnCNN\cite{Zhang2017}, a deep convolutional neural network designed to remove Gaussian noise. DnCNN demonstrates the ability to handle blind Gaussian denoising, where the noise level is unknown, and can be extended to other image restoration tasks such as super-resolution. Other CNN-based methods have been proposed, including FFDNet\cite{Zhang2018}, which achieves fast and flexible denoising by incorporating noise level information into the network. CNNs are applied in other fundamental computer vision tasks like semantic segmentation. Another approach that utilizes noise level information is CFMNet which proposes a more sophisticated method using multi-layer conditional feature modulation, where information from both the noisy image and the noise map is used to dynamically adjust features throughout the network, achieving a better balance between noise removal and detail preservation\cite{du2020}. Some approaches utilize CNNs along with scene classification approaches to train scene-specialized segmentation models\cite{Shaaban2020}.

In addition to Gaussian noise, impulse noise, such as salt-and-pepper noise, is another common type of noise that can severely degrade image quality. Traditional methods for salt-and-pepper noise removal often involve median-based filters or adaptive approaches that exploit local image statistics\cite{Memis2021}. Deep learning methods have also been explored for salt-and-pepper noise removal. SeConvNet introduces a new selective convolutional (SeConv) block that effectively restores salt-and-pepper noise corruption in both grayscale and color images, outperforming traditional methods at high noise densities\cite{Rafiee2021}.

An important architecture in deep learning for image processing is the U-Net. This encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections has proven highly effective for tasks that require precise localization, such as segmentation and image-to-image translation. The U-Net's ability to preserve fine details while capturing global context makes it well-suited for image restoration tasks\cite{Wu2022}. For instance, SCUNet employs a U-Net structure but integrates novel Swin Transformer and convolutional (Swin-Conv) blocks to combine local and non-local modeling capabilities. A key goal of this approach is to achieve practical, general-purpose blind denoising for real-world images without prior assumptions about noise type or intensity. To accomplish this, SCUNet is trained with a sophisticated data synthesis pipeline that includes a wide variety of noise types, such as Gaussian, Poisson, and JPEG compression noise, thereby enabling it to generalize effectively to complex, real-world degradations\cite{Zhang2023}.

While CNNs excel at capturing local dependencies in images, they often struggle to model global image context \cite{Ma2022, Chen2022}, which can be crucial for high-quality image restoration. To address this limitation, Transformers, originally developed for natural language processing\cite{vaswani2023}, have been applied to image recognition \cite{dosovitskiy2021} and restoration \cite{chen2021} tasks. Transformers use self-attention to capture global dependencies, making them strong candidates for various restoration tasks. Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have achieved great success in image restoration. Compared to traditional CNNs, they show more robust feature extraction and improved performance when trained with extensive data\cite{Ali2023}.

One notable example is Restormer\cite{Zamir2022}, an efficient Transformer model designed for high-resolution image restoration. Restormer introduced several key designs in its multi-head attention and feed-forward network components to capture long-range pixel interactions while remaining computationally feasible for large images \cite{Ma20222, Ma2024, Ma2023}. Restormer has achieved state-of-the-art performance on various image restoration tasks, including deraining, deblurring, and denoising\cite{Zamir2022}.

The self-attention mechanism, while effective for modeling global context, comes with a significant computational cost, requiring high memory consumption and adding latency. This computational burden can be prohibitive for processing high-resolution images 
particularly on highly constrained edge devices. Consequently, research has focused on developing more efficient Transformer architectures.

A more recent approach involves leveraging generative models, such as denoising diffusion models, as image priors for restoration. The DiffPIR framework, for instance, integrates a Plug-and-Play (PnP) method directly into the diffusion sampling process, using the model as a generative denoiser prior. This approach is effective for general restoration tasks like deblurring and super-resolution, producing high-fidelity results by combining the principled PnP framework with the strong generative capabilities of diffusion models\cite{zhu2023}.

Image denoising is broadly applicable, improving results in medical imaging, remote sensing, consumer photography, and surveillance. Related image enhancement techniques are also vital for specialized applications, such as optimizing visual information for assistive technologies like retinal prostheses\cite{AlAtabany2011}.

This history reveals a clear divergence and an unmet need in the literature. The field has produced: (1) powerful, general-purpose global models like Transformers and, more recently, Diffusion Models \cite{zhu2023}, which are computationally expensive; and (2) highly efficient, specialized CNNs that excel at specific tasks but lack global context and generalizability. There is a need for a single, resource-efficient architecture that synergistically combines the targeted efficiency of specialized modules with the global context awareness of a Transformer.

The proposed MPDenoiseNet (Multi-Path Denoise Net) model builds upon the strengths of existing deep learning approaches for image denoising. It incorporates SeConv blocks from SeConvNet to effectively handle salt-and-pepper noise, introducing novel anisotropic diffusion blocks to further enhance denoising capabilities. Additionally, MPDenoiseNet leverages Transformer blocks, similar to Restormer, to capture long-range pixel interactions. MPDenoiseNet terminates with an asymmetric U-Net AutoEncoder to enhance its ability to capture fine details and global dependencies. By combining these different components, MPDenoiseNet aims to achieve competitive type-agnostic denoising performance while maintaining resource efficiency.
